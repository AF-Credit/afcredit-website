Understanding the numbers

How to read your results

Gross loan is the facility secured against the property, and the figure your LTV is measured on. Net advance is what actually reaches your account on day one: the gross loan, less the arrangement fee, less the interest for the whole term taken upfront. Repayment at redemption is the gross loan plus an exit fee of one month’s interest.

Retained interest is the part that catches people out, because it does not behave like a mortgage. On a mortgage you borrow £300,000, receive £300,000 and pay monthly. Here, borrow £300,000 gross over 9 months at 0.89% and the £24,030 of interest comes off on day one, alongside a £6,000 arrangement fee. You receive £269,970, pay nothing each month, and repay £300,000 plus a £2,670 exit fee at the end. Valuation and legal costs are paid separately.

So size the deal against the net advance, not the gross loan. If you need £300,000 in the account you have to borrow more than £300,000 — the calculator works forwards from the gross figure, so move the LTV until the net advance matches what the deal actually needs.

Two things move the total most: LTV, since a higher LTV usually carries a higher rate, and term, since interest is charged for every month or part month. There is no early repayment charge, subject to three months’ minimum interest, so redeeming early genuinely costs less.

FAQs

Calculator questions

How is bridging loan interest calculated?

Bridging interest is quoted monthly and calculated on the loan balance. On a £200,000 loan at 0.85% per month, interest is £1,700 per month. AF Credit lends on retained interest: the full term's interest is deducted from your advance on day one, so there are no monthly payments and you repay the gross loan at redemption.

What does LTV mean on a bridging loan?

LTV (loan to value) is the gross loan as a percentage of the property value. At AF Credit the maximum is 65% via AVM valuation, or 75% via desktop valuation or physical inspection. Lower LTVs typically attract lower rates.

What is the difference between gross loan and net advance?

The gross loan is the total facility secured against the property. The net advance is what you actually receive on day one, after retained interest and the arrangement fee are deducted. This calculator shows both, so there are no surprises.

Are these calculator results a loan offer?

No — the figures are indicative and for illustration only. Actual terms depend on the property, LTV, term, exit strategy and underwriting. AF Credit issues real same-day indicative terms on any enquiry, free and without obligation.

What rate should I use in the calculator?
