Understanding the numbers

How to read your results

Gross loan is the total facility secured against the property — the figure your LTV is measured on. Net advance is what actually lands in your account on day one: the gross loan minus the arrangement fee and minus retained interest. Repayment at redemption is what you pay to close the loan — the gross loan, because the interest was already taken on day one.

Two levers move the numbers most: LTV (higher LTV usually means a higher rate) and term (interest accrues monthly, so a realistic-but-short term keeps total cost down). There is no early repayment charge at AF Credit (3-month minimum interest applies), so repaying early reduces your total interest cost.

FAQs

Calculator questions

How is bridging loan interest calculated?

Bridging interest is quoted monthly and calculated on the loan balance. On a £200,000 loan at 0.85% per month, interest is £1,700 per month. AF Credit lends on retained interest: the full term's interest is deducted from your advance on day one, so there are no monthly payments and you repay the gross loan at redemption.

What does LTV mean on a bridging loan?

LTV (loan to value) is the gross loan as a percentage of the property value. At AF Credit the maximum is 65% via AVM valuation, or 75% via desktop valuation or physical inspection. Lower LTVs typically attract lower rates.

What is the difference between gross loan and net advance?

The gross loan is the total facility secured against the property. The net advance is what you actually receive on day one, after retained interest and the arrangement fee are deducted. This calculator shows both, so there are no surprises.

Are these calculator results a loan offer?

No — the figures are indicative and for illustration only. Actual terms depend on the property, LTV, term, exit strategy and underwriting. AF Credit issues real same-day indicative terms on any enquiry, free and without obligation.
