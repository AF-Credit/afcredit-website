The Challenge

Heather Fallon had found an outstanding investment opportunity. A one-bedroom end-terrace bungalow in Hull was available to purchase for £47,000 against an anticipated post-refurbishment value of approximately £85,000 — an 81% uplift on a relatively modest works budget of around £10,000. The numbers were compelling.

The challenges were on the borrower side rather than the property side. She was newly self-employed with limited provable income history, had adverse credit on record, and was working to a hard contractual deadline of 3 September 2026. Conventional mortgage lenders were not an option — not at this speed, and not on this credit profile.

The property was sold via the Modern Method of Auction through iamsold. This method requires the buyer to pay a non-refundable reservation fee — in this case £6,600 — on exchange, in addition to the purchase price. This fee is payable regardless of whether the finance completes and is not recoverable if a lender pulls out.

Critically, Heather had already been through the process twice with other providers. Both times upfront fees had been paid — including approximately £2,000 in legal costs with a previous lender. Both times the finance had failed to materialise. That experience had left her understandably cautious — and she made it clear from the outset that she needed full transparency on costs, likelihood of proceeding, and any potential reasons a deal might not complete, before committing to any further outlay.

How AF Credit Helped

AF Credit assessed the application on the strength of the underlying transaction. At 47% of the anticipated GDV, the loan carried substantial headroom. The property was a straightforward residential bungalow requiring cosmetic refurbishment — not structural works — with a clear sale exit and realistic comparable evidence to support the projected resale value.

Same-day indicative terms were issued on the day of enquiry — 18 August 2026. We were clear from the outset on all costs involved and confirmed that the deal was one we could support, allowing the borrower to proceed with confidence.

An AVM was instructed immediately, removing the need for an upfront physical valuation fee — the cost that had caught Heather out previously. All arrangement fees and monthly interest were structured to roll into the gross facility of £40,000, with no sums required from the borrower prior to completion. The net advance was available in full on the completion date.

Report on title was received on Friday 28 August. Completion followed on 3 September 2026, exactly on the contractual deadline — sixteen days after the first enquiry was submitted via the AF Credit portal at 16:05 on 18 August.

The Outcome

The purchase completed on deadline. The borrower received her net advance with no prior outgoings, having paid nothing upfront. The bridging facility provides the time and capital structure to carry out the planned refurbishment — with the loan to be repaid from the sale of the completed property.

The anticipated sale at approximately £85,000 against a total cost base (purchase plus refurb) of approximately £57,000 represents a gross profit position before finance costs that is clear, realistic and supported by local comparable evidence.

Why a Non-Status Bridging Loan Was the Right Solution

Conventional mortgage lenders rely heavily on provable income and credit scoring. When either falls short of their criteria — regardless of the underlying quality of the investment — the application fails.

A non-status bridging loan from AF Credit assesses the deal on its own merits: the security, the LTV, and the exit. In this case:

  • The purchase price was well below the anticipated post-refurbishment value.
  • The LTV against GDV was just 47% — comfortably within our lending criteria.
  • The refurbishment was cosmetic, low-risk and within a modest budget.
  • The sale exit was realistic and supported by local market evidence.
  • The deadline was achievable — and we met it.
