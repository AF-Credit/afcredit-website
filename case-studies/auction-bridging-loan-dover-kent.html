Why a Modern Method of Auction Reservation Needs Funding Fast

The borrower — a limited company whose sole director is an experienced property investor — had reserved a semi-detached house in Dover $1 A Modern Method of Auction bridging loan has to clear one hurdle before any other: producing a decision in principle for the auction reservation inside the platform’s window.

Modern Method sales do not work like a traditional auction room, but the pressure is comparable. A non-refundable reservation fee is paid up front, the reservation period is short, and the platform expects the buyer to evidence funding almost immediately. The borrower put it plainly: he needed a decision in principle he could send to the auction platform within 24 hours.

That is where a lot of auction buyers come unstuck. A lender who says "we should be able to help" and produces a document a week later has already failed. The reservation is either evidenced on time or it is at risk, and the fee that has already been paid goes with it.

Getting a Decision in Principle Out Inside 24 Hours

Terms went out the same morning, sized at 75% of the £160,000 auction price — a gross facility of £120,000. The decision in principle followed well inside the 24 hours the platform required.

We sized against the auction price. On an auction purchase, a competitive sale is good evidence of market value on the day, and we lend against the lower of price or valuation. Pricing to the figure the market had just set meant no argument about value and no delay waiting to settle one.

Dual representation. One firm acted on both the finance and the purchase. On a sale with a reservation clock, removing a second firm from the chain removes a week of duplicated review.

A limited company borrower was not an obstacle. We lend to companies and SPVs as readily as to individuals. The structure was a first charge with a debenture and a personal guarantee from the director, who took independent legal advice on the guarantee before signing — the normal, proper process rather than a hurdle.

On Fees — the Conversation Most Lenders Avoid

The borrower read the terms and asked us to remove the 0.95% exit fee, pointing out it would make the offer more attractive.

We said no, and explained why: on a facility of this size the economics do not support dropping it. What we did instead was fix the legal fee at £1,900 and absorb the difference ourselves, so the borrower got real money back on the deal rather than a vague promise.

That is a small exchange, but it is the one that matters. A lender who agrees to everything at terms stage and rediscovers its pricing at offer stage has cost the borrower weeks. We would rather give a straight answer in the first conversation and find value somewhere we actually can.

Then the Waiting

The funding was ready within days. The transaction was not.

The seller's solicitor took ten weeks to work through enquiries. Our solicitor chased at the end of July, again in early August, again in late August, and twice more in September. Each time the answer was the same: still waiting on the other side.

There is no clever underwriting that fixes that. What a lender can do is hold its nerve and its pricing. The terms issued in July were the terms that completed in September — no repricing, no renegotiation, no withdrawal because the file had aged. The borrower never had to go back to the market and start again.

Auction buyers tend to assume the risk sits with the lender being slow. Often it sits with the other side of the transaction, and what matters then is whether your lender is still there and still at the same price when the delay finally clears.
